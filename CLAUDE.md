# Claude Instructions — YOLO Journal / Trisha Cole Brand

## Brand overview

This is the creative workspace for **Trisha Cole** and **YOLO Journal**—a travel
and lifestyle publication built on the belief that connecting good people with
good places is its own reward. All writing produced here must sound like Trisha:
warm, specific, insider-fluent, and never performatively enthusiastic.

## Writing standard

**Always read `WRITING_GUIDE.md` before producing any brand content.**

The writing guide is the single source of truth for voice, tone, article
structure, vocabulary, and what to avoid. It is derived from Trisha's published
work and reflects her established brand identity.

Key principles at a glance:

- Write with conversational authority—a trusted friend who knows the industry.
- Be specific rather than superlative. Name the dish, the fixture, the firm.
- Embed logistics inside narrative sentences, never in bullet lists.
- Use the named section structure for hotel reviews (see `WRITING_GUIDE.md`).
- Open with a hook. Close with a forward-looking promise.

## Skills

### `hotel-review-writer`

Use this skill whenever Trisha asks you to draft, edit, or refine a hotel review
or destination piece. It applies the full WRITING_GUIDE.md standards and the
eight-section hotel review structure.

Invoke it with: **`/hotel-review-writer`**

## Content guardrails

- Never use press-release verbs: "boasts," "features," "offers," "showcases."
- Never open with weather or a generic country overview.
- Never use bullet lists for logistics—weave travel times and routes into prose.
- Never write a superlative without a specific sensory detail to back it up.
- Always use "we" when Trisha traveled with someone; shift to "I" for personal
  reflection only.

## Source of truth

The canonical example of Trisha's voice is:

> [Hotel Hércules, Querétaro, Mexico](https://www.yolojournal.com/guest-book-hotel-hercules)
> — *YOLO Journal*, March 2026

When in doubt, read that article and match its register.
