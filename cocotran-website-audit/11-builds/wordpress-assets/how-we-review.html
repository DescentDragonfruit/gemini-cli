How I choose what to write about

I don't write up everywhere I stay, and I don't stay everywhere I'm offered. A place earns a piece when it has a real sense of itself and I would send a client there without a second thought. That's the bar. Not the price, not the name on the gate, but whether someone poured themselves into it.

Most hotels here sit above $800 a night, because that is where my clients travel and where the difference between good and extraordinary actually shows. A few sit below it. They earned their place another way.

What a stay involves

I sleep in the room I write about, and I tell you which one. I eat in the restaurants. I book the treatment. I walk the distance to town with the bag I'd actually carry. I read the bill at checkout and compare it with what the rate promised.

If I didn't do something, I say so. A restaurant that was closed stays closed in the piece. I won't describe a spa from the brochure.

First-hand, or sourced, and which is which

What I saw, ate, paid and noticed is written in the first person. Facts I couldn't see for myself, the architect, the opening year, the number of suites, the chef's last kitchen, come from the hotel, its press office, or a conversation with the general manager, and the piece says where. When I can't confirm a detail, I leave it out. I would rather give you one fewer fact than one wrong one.

Every piece carries a Facts checked date. That is the last time I or my desk went back through the prices, the hours and the names with the hotel's own sources.

Paid stays, hosted stays

Some stays I pay for. Some are hosted or discounted by the hotel, usually because I'm there as an advisor on an inspection trip or as a photographer. Every piece states which, in the line under the title.

A hosted stay is written exactly like a paid one. The hotel does not see the piece before it runs, does not approve it, and does not get the flaw taken out. If I couldn't write it honestly, I wouldn't write it at all.

What “book it through me” means

I also run Coco Tran Travel, where I plan trips for a small number of clients. Many of the hotels on this site can be booked through it, at the same rate you would pay the hotel directly, with the extras that come from the hotel's preferred-partner programmes: breakfast, a credit, an upgrade when one is free, the room I'd ask for.

That relationship is stated wherever it applies. The magazine and the practice stay separate. You can read every word here without ever booking, and book without ever subscribing. No hotel can pay to appear, and nothing here is sponsored.

Affiliate links

A few links earn a small commission if you book through them. They never change what I recommend. The full list is on the Disclosures page.

Keeping it current

Hotels change. Chefs leave, pools get rebuilt, rates move. Each piece shows when I visited and when it was last updated. Updated means something in it actually changed; the original visit date stays so you can judge how fresh the experience is. When I go back, the piece is rewritten, not patched.

If you know something is out of date, tell me: hello@cocotran.com. Corrections go in within the week, and the piece says what changed.

The photographs

Every image is mine unless the caption says otherwise. I shoot on film, because that is how I see, and because a photograph I took is one you can trust is real. Where a hotel's own photographs appear, usually in a roundup of places I haven't stayed, they are credited to the hotel. Captions and alt text describe what is actually in the frame.

To license a photograph, that is Coco Tran Studio.

Where the tools fit

I use software to gather my notes, to check facts against the hotel's own sources, and to keep the site in order. It does not stay in the rooms, take the photographs, or hold the opinions. Nothing is published that I haven't read, and every recommendation is mine to defend.
