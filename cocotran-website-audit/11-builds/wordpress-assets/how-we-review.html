How I choose what to write about

I write about the places I can't stop talking about. A hotel earns a piece when it has a real sense of itself and I would send a client there tomorrow with a huge smile. That's the bar, and it's a joyful one: not the price, not the name on the gate, but whether someone poured their heart into it and you can feel it the moment you arrive.

Most of the hotels here sit above $800 a night, because that's where my clients travel and where the gap between good and extraordinary really shows. A few sit below it, and I love those even more, because they earned their place with nothing but care.

What a stay involves

I sleep in the room I write about, and I tell you which one so you can ask for it. I eat in the restaurants, I book the treatment, I walk into town with the bag I'd actually carry, and I read the bill at checkout against what the rate promised. The small things are where a hotel shows you who it is, and I collect them happily.

If I didn't get to something, I say so. A restaurant that was closed stays closed in the piece. I'd never describe a spa from a brochure when I can come back and try it myself.

First-hand, or sourced, and which is which

What I saw, ate, paid and noticed is written in the first person, because I was there and I want you to feel it. Facts I couldn't see for myself, the architect, the opening year, the number of suites, the chef's last kitchen, come from the hotel, its press office, or a lovely long conversation with the general manager, and the piece says so. When I can't confirm a detail, I leave it out. I'd rather give you one fewer fact than one wrong one.

Every piece carries a Facts checked date. That's the last time my desk and I went back through the prices, the hours and the names with the hotel's own sources, so what you're reading is as current as I can make it.

Paid stays, hosted stays

Some stays I pay for. Some are hosted or discounted by the hotel, usually because I'm there as an advisor on an inspection trip or as a photographer. Every piece tells you which, in the line right under the title, because you deserve to know and I'm proud to be clear about it.

A hosted stay is written exactly like a paid one. The hotel doesn't see the piece before it runs, doesn't approve it, and doesn't get the flaw taken out. If I couldn't write it honestly, I wouldn't write it at all. Happily, the places I choose rarely give me that problem.

What “book it through me” means

This is my favourite part. I also run Coco Tran Travel, where I plan trips for a small number of clients, and many of the hotels on this site can be booked through it. You pay the same rate you'd pay the hotel directly, and you get the extras that come from the hotel's preferred-partner programmes: breakfast, a credit to spend, an upgrade when one is free, and the room I'd ask for, because I've already asked.

That relationship is stated wherever it applies. The magazine and the practice stay separate: you can read every word here without ever booking, and book without ever subscribing. No hotel can pay to appear, and nothing here is sponsored. The recommendations are mine, and I'm delighted to stand behind every one.

Affiliate links

A few links earn a small commission if you book through them. They never change what I recommend, and they help keep the lights on so I can keep travelling and sharing. The full list is on the Disclosures page.

Keeping it current

Hotels change, and that's part of the fun. Chefs move, pools get rebuilt, rates shift. Each piece shows when I visited and when it was last updated. Updated means something in it actually changed; the original visit date stays, so you can judge how fresh the experience is. When I go back, the piece is rewritten with everything I learned, not patched.

If you know something's out of date, I'd genuinely love to hear it: hello@cocotran.com. Corrections go in within the week, and the piece says what changed.

The photographs

Every image is mine unless the caption says otherwise. I shoot on film, because that's how I see, and because a photograph I took is one you can trust is real, light and grain and all. Where a hotel's own photographs appear, usually in a roundup of places I haven't stayed yet, they're credited to the hotel. Captions and alt text describe what's actually in the frame.

To license a photograph, that's Coco Tran Studio, and I'd be thrilled.

Where the tools fit

I use software to gather my notes, check facts against the hotel's own sources, and keep the site tidy. It doesn't stay in the rooms, take the photographs, or hold the opinions. Nothing is published that I haven't read, and every recommendation is mine, happily and completely.
