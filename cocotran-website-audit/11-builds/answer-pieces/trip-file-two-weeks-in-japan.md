**The shape of the trip.** Tokyo, the Alps, Kanazawa and Kyoto in fourteen nights, with one ryokan night and no internal flights. In to Haneda, home from Kansai. Couple. Best in November; April works and costs more for a window nobody can promise you.

## What to book first, and how far ahead

In Coco's order, from `the_details_that_make_it_work` and the Primer:

1. **The ryokan with the private onsen** — nine to twelve months out for blossom or autumn dates. This is the piece that sells out first in the whole country.
2. **The flights, open-jaw** — in to Haneda, home from Kansai. Usually costs nothing extra and saves a whole day. [VERIFY: how far ahead for the fare you want]
3. **City hotels** — Tokyo and Kyoto; Kyoto nine to twelve months out for blossom and autumn.
4. **teamLab** — weeks ahead.
5. **Two or three special dinners** — through the hotel concierge; Coco asks for dinner preferences months before a trip rather than weeks, because the eight-to-twelve-seat counters take bookings from hotels, not strangers.
6. **A private guide** — one morning in Tokyo (day 3) and one in Kyoto (day 11). [VERIFY: lead time]
7. **Shinkansen seats** — once the booking window opens [VERIFY: the Route says ninety days; confirm the current JR reservation window]. Essential in Golden Week and Obon, when seats sell out.
8. **Rail pass arithmetic** — this route is one of the few where a pass can still pay, because of the Alps legs. Price it against individual tickets for the actual dates before buying either.
9. **Hire nothing** — no car on this route; takkyubin, trains, taxis and an IC card on the phone do all of it.

## Packing, money and visa — pointers

There is no Japan Dossier on the site yet [VERIFY: the Dossier format exists for Santorini, Lake Como, Vietnam, Hanoi, Da Nang and Paris only; a Japan Dossier would be the natural home for this sheet]. Until one exists, the published source is The Primer: Japan, section "The questions I actually get" (https://cocotran.com/guide/japan-primer/):

- **Packing** — slip-on shoes and socks without holes: you take your shoes off constantly at ryokan, temples, some restaurants and changing rooms, and it is the thing people thank Coco for most. One overnight bag sized for nights 5–10 (Hakone, Takayama, Kanazawa) while the suitcase goes Tokyo → Kyoto by takkyubin. Tattoos: smaller ones covered with a patch; larger work means a ryokan with a private onsen or a kashikiri bath booked by the hour, and say so at booking. Season: November for the autumn colour; April for blossom; avoid Golden Week (roughly 29 April to 5 May), Obon (mid-August) and New Year. [VERIFY: a packing list by season is not in the published text.]
- **Money** — less cash than you used to need, but yes: cards almost everywhere in the cities; small restaurants, shrines, markets and rural buses are still cash. Withdraw at 7-Eleven or Japan Post ATMs, which take foreign cards reliably when bank ATMs will not. Put the rest on a Suica or Pasmo card on your phone, which works on every train, bus and convenience store in the country. The yen has been weak for years; hotels are the cost, and food, trains and entry fees are cheaper than Europe. Takkyubin about two thousand yen a bag; station umbrellas five hundred yen; a station bowl of noodles about seven hundred yen.
- **Visa** — [VERIFY: not in Coco's published text; add to the Japan Dossier when written].
- **Language** — none needed. Signage is bilingual on transport, menus often have pictures, translation apps do the rest; sumimasen and arigatou gozaimasu go a long way.
- **Getting around** — Haneda is twenty minutes from the centre, Narita ninety; Kansai serves Kyoto and Osaka. Tokyo to Kyoto two hours fifteen; Kyoto to Kanazawa about two; Tokyo to Takayama about four via Nagoya. Reserve seats, Green Car over two hours, ekiben on the platform. In Kyoto, walk and take taxis; the train only for Fushimi Inari and Arashiyama.
- **Safety note** — [VERIFY: nothing formal published; the Primer's only line is that you could leave your wallet on a bench and find it there an hour later].
