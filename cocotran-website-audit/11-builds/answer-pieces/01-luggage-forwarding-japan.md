# Is luggage forwarding in Japan worth it, and how does it work?

## Short answer

Yes, on every single move, and it is the best thing nobody tells first-timers. Takkyubin, the overnight luggage service, carries your suitcase from hotel to hotel for about two thousand yen a bag: you hand it in at the front desk before 10am and it is waiting for you the next afternoon. It suits anyone travelling between cities by train, and it is close to essential if your route includes a ryokan night in Hakone or a stretch in the Alps.

## Why

I call it the single biggest quality-of-life improvement available on a Japan trip, and I mean it literally. The trains are wonderful and the stations are not built for wheelie cases. Send the bag ahead and you travel with an overnight bag instead of wrestling a suitcase through a station, buy an ekiben on the platform, and arrive with your hands free.

The mechanics could not be simpler. Every hotel front desk does this without being asked twice. You hand the suitcase over before ten in the morning, pay roughly two thousand yen a bag, and it travels overnight and is sitting in your next room by the following afternoon. On my two-week route I send the big bags straight from Tokyo to Kyoto, then do Hakone, Takayama and Kanazawa with a small bag over my shoulder. When the train pulls into Kyoto two hours after Kanazawa, the suitcase is already there, and that moment never stops being pleasing.

Two things make it work. Pack the overnight bag properly, because you will live out of it for several nights: slip-on shoes and socks without holes, since you will be taking your shoes off constantly at the ryokan, the temples and some restaurants. And remember that the ryokan night is the one you most want to arrive at lightly. You turn up mid-afternoon, change into a yukata, bathe, eat a long kaiseki dinner in your room and sleep on a futon on tatami. A suitcase has no place in that picture.

Set against a trip where the hotels are the real expense, two thousand yen is close to nothing, and the yen has been weak for years. Do this on every move.

## If you're choosing

- **Send it straight through** if your route is the Tokyo–Kyoto corridor: handed over at the desk on the morning you leave.
- **Send it past the middle** if you are adding Hakone, Takayama or Kanazawa: suitcase from Tokyo direct to Kyoto, and three or four nights out of an overnight bag.
- **Allow an extra day** if you are travelling during Golden Week, Obon or New Year, when the whole country moves at once [VERIFY: whether next-afternoon delivery holds on peak dates and on mountain legs].
