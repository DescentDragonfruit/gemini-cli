# Where should I stay in Kyoto for a first visit?

## Short answer

Downtown, around Kawaramachi, Pontocho and Nishiki, is where I put most first-timers: you can walk to dinner, the subway is there, and the city feels alive in the evening. Higashiyama, on the eastern hills, is quieter and lovelier and means a taxi to dinner, so take it if you would rather wake up among the temples. The Ritz-Carlton Kyoto on the Kamo river is the best big hotel in the city, and Sowaka, a converted ryotei of twenty-three rooms in Higashiyama, is my favourite small one.

## Why

Pick the neighbourhood before the hotel. Kyoto is bigger than it looks, Arashiyama to Fushimi Inari is an hour across town, and the subway is only two lines, so where you sleep decides how much of the day you spend getting somewhere. A Kyoto city bus in November is an experience nobody needs. Taxis, happily, are cheap, immaculate and everywhere, and the drivers wear white gloves, which is why staying slightly out is never a disaster, only a small tax on every dinner.

Downtown gives you the river, the lantern-lit alley of Pontocho and Nishiki market on foot. The Ritz-Carlton sits on the Kamo at the edge of it. Hotel The Mitsui has a private onsen fed by its own hot spring and a four-hundred-year-old gate out front. Ace Hotel Kyoto is the fun, much cheaper, design-led option in a renovated telephone exchange.

Higashiyama is the Kyoto in the photographs: the stone-stepped lanes of Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka, Kiyomizu-dera on its wooden stage, Gion as the lanterns come on. Sowaka is there, and Park Hyatt Kyoto sits just below Kiyomizu-dera with the pagoda in the window, a view I have photographed. What you trade is dinner, because you will taxi to most restaurants and come home the same way.

Out of town is for the second trip. Aman Kyoto is serene and remarkable in its forest garden north of the city, and a twenty-five-minute taxi from anything. Hoshinoya Kyoto at Arashiyama is reached by private boat up the Hozu river, a wonderful way to arrive and an awkward way to pop out for dinner.

Whichever you choose, book nine to twelve months out for blossom and autumn, and let the hotel make your kaiseki reservations, because many of the counters will not take a stranger's booking.

## If you're choosing

- **Downtown** if it is your first time, you like walking to dinner, and you want the evening to have somewhere to go.
- **Higashiyama** if you are an early riser who wants the stone lanes at six with nobody in them, and do not mind a taxi home.
- **Aman or Hoshinoya** if this is your second Kyoto and you want the hotel to be the day.
- **Near Kyoto Station** only if you are using Kyoto as a rail base; it is convenient and charmless, and I would rather you did not.
