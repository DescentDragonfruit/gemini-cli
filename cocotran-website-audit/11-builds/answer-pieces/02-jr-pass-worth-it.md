# Is the JR Pass still worth it?

## Short answer

Often not any more. The Japan Rail Pass went up sharply in October 2023, and for most two-week trips that stay between Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, individual tickets now work out cheaper. Where it can still pay is a route that swings through the Alps, Tokyo to Hakone, Takayama, Kanazawa and Kyoto, so do the arithmetic for your actual legs rather than buying it out of habit.

## Why

The pass used to be an automatic purchase and I understand why people still reach for it. The trouble is that the classic first trip, the corridor between Tokyo and Kyoto with a bullet train down the middle, does not involve enough train. Tokyo to Kyoto is two hours fifteen. Add Osaka, fifteen minutes from Kyoto on the Shinkansen, and a day return to Nara, forty minutes away, and you have spent far less on tickets than the pass now costs. Since the 2023 price rise it no longer pays for itself on that standard shape.

The route where I still run the numbers properly is the one I build most often for a fortnight: Tokyo, then Hakone for the ryokan night, then into the Alps for Takayama, across to Kanazawa, and down to Kyoto. Kyoto to Kanazawa is about two hours and Tokyo to Takayama about four via Nagoya, and it is those mountain legs that tip the balance. On that itinerary a pass can genuinely come out ahead. Price it against point-to-point fares for your dates rather than assuming either way; the answer changes with the route, not with the length of the trip.

Whichever way you go, reserve your Shinkansen seats, and take the Green Car on anything over two hours. Heading west out of Tokyo, ask for the right-hand side of the carriage and you get Mount Fuji about forty minutes out. If you are travelling in Golden Week or Obon, the seats themselves are what sell out, so book them the moment your window opens [VERIFY: current reservation window; the Route says ninety days].

One more saving nobody asks for: fly open-jaw, into Haneda and home from Kansai. It costs almost nothing extra and spares you a whole day of backtracking, pass or no pass.

## If you're choosing

- **Skip the pass** if you are doing Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, with or without Nara and Hakone: buy individual tickets and reserve seats.
- **Price it properly** if your route includes Takayama and Kanazawa: the Alps legs are where a pass can still win.
- **Buy seats early either way** if your dates touch Golden Week (roughly 29 April to 5 May), Obon in mid-August or New Year.
- **Spend the difference on the Green Car** for the long legs; it is the better use of the money on the corridor trip.
