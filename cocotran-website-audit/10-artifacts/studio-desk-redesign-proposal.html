UX review · Studio Desk · 27 October 2026

One door for each job. One path for each piece.

The desk can do a lot now, but every feature landed wherever it was asked for, so the same job shows up in five places and every screen asks you to decide something. This is a plan to make it feel like an editor's desk again: calm, in order, with one obvious next step.

What makes it feel busy

The problem, measured

Counted in the current desk (version 65) with your sample data.

4ways to move around: the top pills, the More menu, the bottom bar and a floating Sections button. They use three different numbering systems: Trips is I in its heading, II in the bottom bar and ✦ in the menu.
17destinations, all weighted the same. House rules, Playbooks and the AI benchmark sit beside Today and Trips.
13tabs inside Trips alone, with 194 buttons and 49 dropdowns on a page about 7,700 pixels tall.
7places answer "what do I do first": Today, Next 3, the Overview AI, the trip priority call, each trip's path, the Ideas path card and the weekly brief. They can disagree.
5different filter bars, each with 6–9 dropdowns and its own options.
12words for where a piece is: planned, queued, needs-coco, needs you, drafting, Cull, Edit, Write, Share, Done, missing, live.
~350pxof title, date and explanation before any work on most pages. The Authority map opens with a 650-character paragraph.
1button style for almost everything. "Open", "Cull now", "Ask the desk" and "Import now" are all solid black, so nothing stands out as the next step.
The new map

Rooms in the order the work happens

Your real flow is trip → photos → writing → publishing → sharing. The desk should be laid out the same way, left to right, with Today as the front door that only ever shows your next step. Everything that exists today keeps a home; it just stops competing for attention.

The editorial system

Six rules every screen follows

A short header

A small label, a 40px title and one line saying what the page is for. The explanations move behind a "How this works" link.

WriteWriteWhat's ready to write, and what's with the worker.

One primary button

A solid button is the next step. Everything else is outlined or a text link. If a card has two solid buttons, one of them is wrong.

Start the cullPick myselfnot today

One card shape

Every card has a label (place · type), a serif title, one meta line, the stepper or a status pill, and one action, always in the same places.

Brazil · VerdictCullLive

One filter pattern

A search box and three chips that matter for that room, then "More filters" in a sheet. Your filters are remembered per room, and you can save a view ("Brazil, in season").

CountryPrioritySeasonMore filters

Show five, hide the rest

Lists open with the top five and a "show all 42". Archive and finished trips fold away by default, so long pages stop being long.

1 · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5show all 42

Type does the work

Serif for titles only, sans for everything you operate. Four sizes, an 8-point spacing grid, and colour only for status: green done, amber waiting, red late.

TitleCard titleBody and buttonsLabel
For the overwhelmed days

Built to help you keep going

Focus mode

One tap hides the whole desk except the task in front of you, with a 25-minute timer. When you mark it done, the next one appears, and nothing else does.

A finish line each week

When the week's 2 articles and 3 posts are done, Today says so and stops suggesting more. Stopping counts as done.

One engine, one answer

The ranking that picks your one thing also orders every room, so Today, the trip path and the board always agree. No more seven answers to the same question.

"Not today" is allowed

Skipping a task moves it down without guilt or a red badge. The desk only flags things that miss a real deadline, like a search season closing.

How we get there

Four steps, each one a single update

Nothing is deleted along the way. Every feature you asked for keeps working; it moves to its room and gets calmer.

Phase 1Calm
  • One navigation: Today · Trips · Photos · Write · Publish · Share · Grow, plus "+ New". The same order on the phone's bottom bar. No roman numerals, no floating Sections button.
  • Short headers, with explanations behind "How this works".
  • The button hierarchy and the one status language everywhere.
The biggest drop in noise for the least risk.
Phase 2One answer
  • Today becomes the only "what first", fed by one ranking engine, with the weekly finish line.
  • Trips slims to Overview plus one page per trip; Next 3, This week, Writing and Board move out.
You open the desk and know what to do in five seconds.
Phase 3Rooms
  • The new Photos room: one cull queue, Your eye, Ready.
  • The Write board, with New piece and the queue folded in.
  • Publish, Share and Grow as their own rooms; House rules and Playbooks move to Settings.
The desk follows your flow from trip to post.
Phase 4Polish
  • Focus mode, the shared filter pattern and saved views.
  • "Show five" lists and folded archives everywhere, plus a phone pass on every room.
The desk is pleasant to use every day.
