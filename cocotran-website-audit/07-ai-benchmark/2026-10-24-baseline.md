No access to ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google AI Overviews in this run; treat the result as a proxy for what a retrieval-based answer engine would see and cite. Question set and repeatable method: `questions.md`.

## Observations

- **Zero visibility is a retrieval problem, not a content problem.** Site-restricted searches show cocotran already has on-topic pieces for at least 6 of 20 questions (Q1, 3, 6, 8, 10, 14) — the La Residencia and Caruso reviews are better matches than anything cited. They are indexed but rank below OTAs, aggregators and mid-tier blogs, so an answer engine never sees them.
- **The bot-verification interstitial is the single most damaging finding.** Every fetch of cocotran.com (homepage, robots.txt, two article URLs) returned a Cloudflare-style "request is being verified" page. The main audit's UA tests passed for GPTBot/PerplexityBot/ClaudeBot, so the challenge is likely triggered by non-browser/datacenter fetchers or a managed-challenge rule — which is exactly how AI answer engines and citation crawlers fetch pages. Until this is fixed, even a ranking page cannot be read, quoted or cited. Verify in Cloudflare Security → Bots / WAF managed challenge; allow verified bots.
- **Competitors expose full text; cocotran exposes ~250–350 words before a wall** (audit §D). Snippets in search results for competitors were specific (prices, scores, room counts, dates). An engine synthesising from the lead-in alone has less to quote than from a Trip.com review page.
- **No Article/Review schema, author or dates on the 75 new pieces** (audit §C1) vs competitors carrying BlogPosting/Review + dates. Several cocotran titles carry "(2026)" while snippets show ~900–1,000-day-old content and a "3 Week Japan Itinerary … 2023" title — freshness signals conflict.
- **"X vs Y" and "is X worth it" formats win hotel queries.** Luxury Travel Diary (Q2), TripExpert (Q7), Japlanease (Q16) rank with explicit comparison titles. cocotran's Property/Verdict format is well suited; titles and H1s should match the question shape ("Caruso, A Belmond Hotel review: is it worth it?").
- **The brand query is lost to Virtuoso.** Q20 should be the easiest win: an About page with Person schema (`sameAs`, `jobTitle`, `knowsAbout`, affiliation), consistent host-agency naming, and the homepage not behind a challenge. Fix in `03-fixes/schema-person-organization.json` and `about-page-trust-block.md`.
- **Weak fields exist where firsthand authority would win fast:** Q4 (firsthand reviews — only self-descriptions ranked), Q12 (Hanoi — 2016 recaps and TripAdvisor), Q6/Q8 (OTAs, no strong editorial voice). These are the highest-leverage targets for the first re-run.
- **Generic "where to stay" and informational questions (Q5, 11, 13, 15, 17, 18, 19) are saturated by volume bloggers and affiliates**; cocotran should not expect to displace them without destination hubs (see `destination-hub-spec.md`) and should prioritise property/verdict and brand queries first.

## Limitations of this run

- No direct access to ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Google AI Overviews; results reflect one web index (Claude Code WebSearch, US locale, standard mode) plus Claude's synthesis. Real engines use different indexes, re-ranking and freshness windows.
- Result positions were not always visible; "appeared / did not appear" is reliable, ordinal rank is not.
- One run per query, one day; search results fluctuate. Treat changes smaller than ±2 questions on the next run as noise.
- cocotran pages could not be fetched (interstitial), so content-accuracy checks (step d) were impossible and the "correct article?" column is inferred from site-restricted result titles/snippets only.
- Keyword forms were chosen once; keep them fixed (listed in `questions.md`) for comparability.
- Competitor tallies count distinct questions, not result slots, except where noted.

*Next run: repeat after the Cloudflare challenge, sitemap/CPT, schema and paywall-markup fixes ship; target first movement on Q6, Q8, Q20, then Q1, Q3, Q4.*
